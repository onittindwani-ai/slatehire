# The four fifths rule

Checked 2026-11-23. 29 CFR 1607.4(D), the US Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1607.4

- VERIFIED: a selection rate for a group that is less than four fifths (80 percent) of the rate of the group with the highest rate is generally regarded by the federal agencies as evidence of adverse impact. The same paragraph says smaller differences can still be adverse impact when they are significant in statistical and practical terms, so it is not a pass or fail line.
- In this project: the fairness page prints exact fractions, and compares groups only when each has at least 10 people. It is an indicator for a human to look at, not legal advice. Rules elsewhere differ.
